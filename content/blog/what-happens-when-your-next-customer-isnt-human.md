# What Happens When Your Next Customer Isn't Human?

The last time you booked a hotel, you probably opened a dozen tabs.

Next time, you might not open any.

You'll just say: *find me a quiet hotel in Lisbon under 150 euros.*

And something else will do the looking for you.

It will never see the beautiful photos. It won't read the slogan. It won't feel a thing.

**But it will decide which three hotels you ever get to see.**

Millions of people already shop this way. Sometimes the assistant even pays.

We still make the final choice. But more and more, **we choose from a list that something else made.**

## The customer was always human

Every time customers found a new way to reach us, business changed with them.

When people walked, we built shop windows.

When they went online, we built websites.

When they picked up phones, we rebuilt everything for a small screen.

Each time, the customer was still a person.

**This time, it may not be.**

And a lot of business quietly depends on that. Supermarkets put the milk at the back, so we walk past everything else. Sales end at midnight, because we hate missing out. These tricks work because we get distracted, impatient and emotional.

An agent doesn't. It walks straight to the milk. It doesn't care that the sale ends tonight. It just compares the facts.

**A surprising amount of the economy runs on human limits. Agents don't have them.**

That changes a lot more than marketing:

- **Getting noticed matters less than getting on the short list.** If you're not on it, the person behind the agent will never know you exist.
- **Persuasion stops working.** Urgency and clever pricing mean nothing to a customer that only compares facts.
- **Your numbers can go quiet.** Many agents never show up in the tools companies use to measure visits.
- **"Are you human?" starts to backfire.** A helpful agent and a fraud bot can look almost the same, so blocking robots can mean turning away buyers.
- **Complaints can come from machines.** Banks have already seen customers' agents sending complaints their owners never approved.
- **Consent gets blurry.** When an agent clicks "I agree," who actually agreed?

## Who is your agent working for?

There is another side to this, and it is ours.

When an assistant shops for us, we save time. But we also give away part of the choosing. We see what it recommends, not everything it looked at, and not why it chose.

And the companies that build these assistants also sell advertising. Google is already bringing ads into its AI answers.

So the most important question may also be the simplest:

**Who is your agent really working for?**

![A person follows their AI assistant, unaware that someone else may be pulling its strings.](/images/journal/who-is-your-agent-working-for.png)

For now, most of us still press "buy" ourselves. That won't last forever.

It is already moving faster than the experts expected. In June, Cloudflare's CEO noticed that on his company's network, AI agents were creating more web traffic than humans. "Welp, that happened faster than I predicted," he wrote.

We have always built businesses around human attention.

Your next customer may not have any.

So make sure you're worth recommending. And make sure the agents recommending things to you are worth trusting.
